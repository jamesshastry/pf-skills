# Charitable giving strategy

**Donating an appreciated security in kind does two things at once.** The full market value is deductible *and* the embedded capital gain is never realised by anyone — the charity is exempt, so the gain evaporates rather than transferring. The deduction is identical whether you give the share or sell it and give the cash, so the avoided gain is pure gain.

**$3,384 of capital gains tax avoided** by giving the positions below in kind rather than selling first.

| Holding | Value | Basis | Gain | Long-term | Deductible | Gain tax avoided | Do this |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Northwind | $22,000 | $4,000 | $18,000 | yes | $22,000 | $3,384 | **give in kind** |
| intl_fund | $6,000 | $9,000 | ($3,000) | yes | $6,000 | **not recorded** | **sell first, donate cash** |
| recent_lot | $8,000 | $5,000 | $3,000 | **no** | $5,000 | **not recorded** | wait, or give something else |

- **Northwind** — $18,000 of unrealised gain on $22,000. Given in kind, the full $22,000 is deductible and the gain is never realised by anyone.

- **intl_fund** — Worth $6,000 against $9,000 of basis. Donating it in kind deducts $6,000 and **forfeits the $3,000 loss permanently** — no one ever claims it. Sell, book the loss against other gains, donate the proceeds: the charity receives the same amount, the deduction is the same, and the loss survives.

- **recent_lot** — Held under a year, so the deduction is limited to **basis** ($5,000), not the $8,000 it is worth. That removes most of the reason to give it in kind. Holding to 366 days converts the deduction to full value; a long-term lot of the same security is the better gift today.

- **$3,384 of capital gains tax avoided** if the appreciated positions above are given in kind rather than sold first. This is money that reaches the charity instead of the IRS at no additional cost to the donor — the deduction is the same either way, so the avoided gain is pure gain.

- **Transfer the shares, do not sell and wire the cash.** The whole benefit sits in the security arriving at the charity intact. A sale the day before, however briefly, realises the gain and there is no way back from it. Most donor-advised funds accept in-kind transfers; many small charities cannot, which is one of the reasons a DAF exists.

## Bunching

|  |  |
|---|---|
| Standard deduction | $30,000 |
| Other itemizable deductions | $12,000 |
| Giving, per year | $9,000 |
| Best window | 5 years |
| Deductions taken — giving annually | $150,000 |
| Deductions taken — bunched | $177,000 |
| Benefit at 24% | $6,480 |

- **Bunching 5 years of giving into one is worth $6,480** at a 24% marginal rate — $27,000 of additional deduction over the 5-year window. Giving $9,000 a year against a $30,000 standard deduction wastes most of it: the gift has to clear the floor before it is worth anything, and annually it does not.

- **A donor-advised fund separates the deduction from the grant.** Fund it in the bunch year, take the deduction that year, and grant to the charities on the same schedule as before. The charity's cash flow does not have to absorb the bunching — which is the objection that stops most households doing it.

## AGI limits

- Against $180,000 of AGI: up to $108,000 of cash (60%) and $54,000 of appreciated property at fair market value (30%) are deductible this year. **The two limits are different**, and a bunched in-kind gift sized against the cash limit overshoots by half.

## Qualified charitable distributions

- **Not yet eligible** — a QCD requires age 70.5, and the oldest recorded adult is 41. Worth knowing about early: it becomes the most efficient way to give for a household that takes the standard deduction, because the distribution never enters AGI at all.

## Before acting

- **Confirm the charity accepts in-kind transfers.** Most donor-advised funds do; many small charities cannot, which is one of the reasons a DAF exists.
- **Initiate the transfer early.** A share transfer settles on its own schedule, and a gift that lands on 2 January is deductible in the wrong year.
- **Give the specific long-term lot**, identified to the broker. A default lot selection can hand over the short-term one, which is deductible at basis.
