# Withdrawal sequencing

## The conventional order, and why it's not enough

Taxable → tax-deferred → Roth. The logic is sound: spend the lightly taxed
money first, let the sheltered accounts compound, leave the never-taxed account
for last.

**But strict sequencing is wrong for most households, in a specific way.** It
leaves the low brackets empty during early retirement, then forces large
ordinary-income withdrawals once RMDs begin. Every individual step looked
tax-efficient; the lifetime total is higher.

## Fill brackets, don't drain accounts

1. Spend from taxable for cash flow.
2. Each year, compute the headroom to the top of the target bracket.
3. Fill it with tax-deferred withdrawals or Roth conversions.
4. Leave the Roth alone as long as possible.

**Unused bracket space does not carry forward.** A year spent entirely in the
lowest bracket has permanently wasted the headroom above it. That is the whole
argument, and it is why the answer is a blend rather than a sequence.

## Pre-59½ access is a plan, not a number

Retiring early needs a route to the money, and the two that matter:

- **Rule of 55** — separation from service in or after the year you turn 55,
  **workplace plan only**.
- **§72(t)** substantially equal periodic payments, which are rigid once
  started.

**Rolling a 401(k) into an IRA forfeits the Rule of 55.** It is a common
mistake, it is irreversible, and it is usually made for good-sounding reasons
(lower fees, more choice) by someone who has not yet decided when they are
retiring. Raise it before the rollover, not after.

## Asset location matters as much as order

Income-producing assets belong in tax-deferred accounts. The highest
expected-return assets belong in the Roth, because that is where growth is
never taxed. Getting this wrong costs more over a long retirement than getting
the withdrawal order wrong.

## What this doesn't give you

Bracket thresholds, standard deduction amounts, and capital-gains breakpoints
are year-specific and **not modelled here**. This skill gives the structure;
the numbers come from the current year's tables. Don't invent them.

## Closing

1. **The order**, then immediately the reason it's a starting point.
2. **The bracket-filling alternative**, concretely.
3. **Access routes**, if retirement before 59½ is in play — and the rollover
   warning.
4. **Asset location**, which is usually unexamined.
