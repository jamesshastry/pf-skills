# HSA review

## Why it ranks so high

**The only triple-tax-advantaged account available**: deductible going in,
untaxed while it grows, untaxed coming out for qualified medical expenses.
Every other account gives up one of the three. That is why it sits second in
the contribution order, above elective deferral and behind only the employer
match.

## The part most people miss

**An HSA is a retirement account that happens to pay for healthcare.**

Used as a spending account it's merely a good deal. Used as an investment
account it's the best one available — and the difference is three habits:

- **Pay current medical costs out of pocket** where cash flow allows, and leave
  the balance invested.
- **Keep the receipts.** There is no deadline for reimbursing yourself for a
  qualified expense. A receipt from today can be reimbursed decades from now,
  tax-free, after the balance has compounded — effectively an unlimited-duration
  tax-free withdrawal option.
- **Actually invest the balance.** Most custodians default to a cash sweep and
  require an explicit election. A large HSA sitting in cash is the most common
  failure here, and the most silent.

## Two facts about the long game

**After 65, non-medical withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income without
penalty.** So the downside case for over-funding is that it behaves like a
traditional IRA — which makes over-funding a mild risk, not a real one.

**An HSA inherited by anyone other than a spouse is fully taxable in a single
year.** That makes it a poor asset to leave behind, and an argument for
spending it down before less-taxed assets late in life. A spouse can inherit it
as their own HSA; nobody else can.

## If not eligible

Eligibility requires enrolment in a qualifying high-deductible health plan, so
the real decision happens at open enrolment.

**The comparison people get wrong is premium-only.** A HDHP usually pairs a
lower premium with a higher deductible, and the HSA tax benefit is a third term
that belongs in the arithmetic. Compare total expected annual cost including
the tax saving — and run it against a *bad* health year, not an average one,
since the whole point of insurance is the bad case.

## Closing

1. **Unused space**, if eligible, framed as the most tax-efficient dollars
   available.
2. **Whether the balance is invested or sitting in cash** — ask, because the
   facts file doesn't carry it.
3. **The receipt-banking habit**, if it isn't already in place.
4. If not eligible, **the open-enrolment date**, since that is the only moment
   the decision can be made.
